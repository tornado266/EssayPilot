## 7. Band 7.5 Rewrite

There is ongoing debate about whether university students should concentrate solely on their chosen major or explore subjects outside their main field. Although both approaches offer benefits, I believe students should prioritise their major while retaining limited access to optional courses.

On the one hand, specialisation allows students to develop the depth of knowledge required for demanding careers. Medical students, for example, must master a large amount of theory while completing practical training. If too much time is devoted to unrelated modules, they may struggle to build the clinical competence expected of future professionals. A strong focus on the major can therefore improve both academic performance and career preparation.

On the other hand, studying beyond one discipline can broaden students’ perspectives and strengthen transferable skills. A business student who takes an introductory programming course may develop analytical abilities that are useful in data-driven workplaces. Exposure to other fields can also encourage creative thinking by helping students connect ideas that would otherwise remain separate.

In my view, however, breadth should support rather than replace specialisation. Optional modules are worthwhile when they occupy a limited part of the timetable and complement the student’s main goals. This arrangement gives learners room to explore without weakening the expertise that their degree is designed to provide.

In conclusion, students benefit from both depth and variety, but their major should remain the priority. Universities should therefore offer carefully selected electives alongside a strong core curriculum.
