# IELTS Writing Task 2 band descriptors (working reference)

Official basis: IELTS Writing Band Descriptors, updated May 2023.

Source: https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-writing-band-descriptors.pdf

This is an independently written operational summary. When wording or interpretation is uncertain, consult the linked official descriptor. A response must be matched holistically within each criterion; isolated signals do not determine a band.

## Task Response

| Band | Operational summary |
|---:|---|
| 9 | Fully addresses the prompt with a clear, fully developed position; ideas are directly relevant, deeply extended, and well supported. |
| 8 | Addresses the prompt sufficiently and presents a clear, well-developed position; relevant ideas are well extended and supported, with only occasional omissions or lapses. |
| 7 | Addresses all parts, maintains a clear developed position, and presents relevant main ideas that are extended and supported, though support may sometimes lack focus or precision. |
| 6 | Addresses the main parts and presents a relevant position, but conclusions may be unclear, repetitive, or insufficiently developed; some ideas need stronger support. |
| 5 | Addresses the task incompletely or unevenly; a position exists but development is not always clear, and main ideas are limited, repetitive, insufficiently supported, or partly irrelevant. |
| 4 | Responds only minimally or tangentially; the position is unclear and ideas are difficult to identify, undeveloped, or irrelevant. |
| 3 | Does not adequately address the prompt and offers no clear position; any ideas are few, largely irrelevant, or not developed. |
| 2 | Barely relates to the task and contains no discernible position or usable development. |
| 1 | Provides no meaningful response to the task. |
| 0 | No assessable response, or the response is entirely unrelated or not written as required. |

## Coherence and Cohesion

| Band | Operational summary |
|---:|---|
| 9 | The message is effortless to follow; cohesion is unobtrusive and skilfully controlled, and paragraphing is managed with complete effectiveness. |
| 8 | Ideas are logically sequenced and easy to follow; cohesion and referencing are well controlled, with only occasional lapses; paragraphs are appropriate. |
| 7 | Information and ideas progress logically; cohesive devices and referencing are generally effective, and each paragraph has a clear central topic, despite minor overuse or underuse. |
| 6 | Overall progression is apparent, but connections may be mechanical, faulty, or unclear; referencing and paragraph focus are not always controlled. |
| 5 | Some organisation is visible, but progression is weak; cohesive devices may be inaccurate or repetitive, and paragraphing may be inadequate. |
| 4 | Ideas are present but are not arranged coherently; basic linking is repetitive or inaccurate, and paragraphing may be confusing or absent. |
| 3 | There is no clear logical organisation; relationships between ideas are hard to follow and paragraphing does not help. |
| 2 | Very little organisation or connection between ideas is evident. |
| 1 | The response contains virtually no coherent message. |
| 0 | No assessable response. |
## Lexical Resource

| Band | Operational summary |
|---:|---|
| 9 | Vocabulary is used with full flexibility and precision; natural, sophisticated control is sustained, and spelling or word-formation slips are extremely rare. |
| 8 | A wide resource is used fluently and flexibly to convey precise meaning; less common language is skilfully handled, with only occasional inaccuracies. |
| 7 | Vocabulary is sufficient for flexibility and precision; some less common items and awareness of style or collocation are evident, though occasional choice, spelling, or formation errors occur. |
| 6 | Vocabulary is adequate for the task and meaning is generally clear; attempts at less common language show some inaccuracy, and spelling or formation errors occur without normally blocking communication. |
| 5 | Range is limited but minimally adequate; noticeable errors in choice, spelling, or formation may cause difficulty for the reader. |
| 4 | Only basic or repetitive vocabulary is available and is often inappropriate; errors can impede meaning. |
| 3 | Vocabulary is extremely limited and control is poor, making the message difficult to understand. |
| 2 | Only isolated words or memorised fragments can be recognised. |
| 1 | Virtually no usable vocabulary is demonstrated. |
| 0 | No assessable response. |

## Grammatical Range and Accuracy

| Band | Operational summary |
|---:|---|
| 9 | A full range of structures is used naturally with complete flexibility and control; errors are extremely rare and minor. |
| 8 | A wide range of structures is used flexibly and accurately; most sentences are error-free and the few errors do not impair communication. |
| 7 | A variety of complex structures is used with generally good control; error-free sentences are frequent, and remaining grammar or punctuation errors do not reduce clarity. |
| 6 | Both simple and complex forms are attempted, but flexibility and control are limited; errors occur, though they rarely prevent understanding. |
| 5 | Structural range is limited and complex forms are often faulty; errors are frequent and can cause some difficulty, although the general meaning is usually recoverable. |
| 4 | Only a small range of structures is used; errors are frequent and may distort or obstruct meaning. |
| 3 | Sentence forms are very limited and persistent errors make the message hard to understand. |
| 2 | Little evidence of sentence-level control is present beyond memorised phrases. |
| 1 | No usable sentence structure is demonstrated. |
| 0 | No assessable response. |
