# Task 2 assessment criteria

Official basis:

- https://ielts.org/take-a-test/preparation-resources/writing-test-resources
- https://ielts.org/cdn/ielts-guides/ielts-writing-key-assessment-criteria.pdf

IELTS Writing Task 2 is assessed through four equally weighted criteria. The application calculates the task result from the four criterion bands.

## Task Response

Judge whether the response addresses every part and instruction in the question, maintains a relevant position, and develops relevant main ideas with explanation or support. The significance of an omission depends on what the prompt requires and how much of the task remains addressed; there is no universal automatic deduction for a partial omission.

## Coherence and Cohesion

Judge the logical ordering and progression of ideas, paragraph focus, referencing, and the range and control of cohesive devices. Linking words are not valuable by their count alone. Conventional paragraphing is useful when it clarifies the argument, but stylistic preference is not a scoring rule.

## Lexical Resource

Judge range, appropriacy, precision, collocation, spelling, and word formation in context. Consider the frequency, density, and communicative effect of limitations. Repeating an unavoidable task term is not automatically a weakness, and no fixed repetition count determines a band.

## Grammatical Range and Accuracy

Judge the range and flexibility of sentence forms together with grammatical and punctuation control. Consider how often errors occur and whether they affect communication. Do not require named constructions such as conditionals, passives, or relative clauses; reward successful range and control actually demonstrated.

## Boundaries

- Memorised or formulaic language matters only insofar as the demonstrated performance matches or fails to match a descriptor. It does not create an automatic score cap.
- Apparent author identity, use of AI, or examiner expectations about AI bias are irrelevant to the descriptor decision.
- Word count and response format may prompt closer inspection of task completion or development, but they do not substitute for criterion-level evidence.
- An unconventional but clear, accurate, and relevant style must not be treated as an error merely because a different style is preferred.
